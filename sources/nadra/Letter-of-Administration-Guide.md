# Letter of Administration — Pakistan

## 1. Overview

A **Letter of Administration (LOA)** is a legal document issued to the legal heirs of a deceased person to establish their authority and entitlement concerning the deceased's **immovable property/assets**.

NADRA is authorized under applicable federal and provincial succession laws to issue Letters of Administration and Succession Certificates to legal heirs.

In general:

- **Letter of Administration** → Immovable property/assets
- **Succession Certificate** → Movable property/assets

The exact procedure can vary by province.

---

## 2. Eligibility

A Letter of Administration may be applied for by the **legal heirs of a deceased person**.

The applicant may:

- Apply personally as a legal heir.
- Authorize one legal heir to submit the application on behalf of all other legal heirs.
- Apply through a legal counsel.

Under the Sindh Letters of Administration and Succession Certificates Act, an application may be filed by the legal heirs or their legal counsel.

---

## 3. When a Letter of Administration Is Required

A Letter of Administration may be required when the deceased person owned **immovable property**, such as:

- House
- Apartment
- Plot
- Land
- Agricultural land
- Other immovable property

The document helps the legal heirs establish their legal entitlement to the deceased's property.

---

## 4. Required Documents

The application generally requires:

### 1. Death Certificate

A valid death certificate of the deceased.

### 2. List of Legal Heirs

A complete list of the legal heirs of the deceased.

### 3. CNICs

Copies of the CNICs/NICOPs or other applicable identity documents of the legal heirs.

### 4. Authorization

Where one legal heir is submitting the application on behalf of the others, an authorization in the prescribed form may be required.

### 5. Property Details

Details of the deceased's immovable property/assets for which the Letter of Administration is being requested.

### 6. Family Information

Relevant family information maintained in NADRA records, including the Family Registration Certificate (FRC), may be used for determining the legal heirs.

> NADRA or the relevant authority may request additional documents or verification depending on the circumstances of the case.

---

## 5. Application Process

### Step 1 — Obtain the Death Certificate

Obtain the official death certificate of the deceased.

---

### Step 2 — Identify All Legal Heirs

Prepare the complete list of legal heirs.

Their identity documents should be available for verification.

---

### Step 3 — Prepare Property Information

Collect the relevant information about the deceased's immovable property.

Examples include:

- Property address
- Plot/house number
- Property documents
- Ownership information
- Relevant registration details

---

### Step 4 — Prepare Authorization

If one legal heir will submit the application on behalf of all other heirs, the other heirs may authorize that person using the prescribed authorization form.

NADRA provides an **Affidavit/Authorization for Letter of Administration/Succession Certificate** form.

---

### Step 5 — Submit Application

The application is submitted to the relevant authority/NADRA Succession Facilitation Unit.

In Sindh, an application may be filed at a notified office having jurisdiction where:

- The deceased ordinarily resided at the time of death; or
- The deceased's property/asset is located.

---

### Step 6 — Submit Required Documents

Attach:

- Death certificate
- List of legal heirs
- Identity documents of legal heirs
- Authorization, where applicable
- Property/asset details

---

### Step 7 — Public Notice

After receiving the application, the authority publishes a notice for the general public.

Under the Sindh procedure, the notice is published on the authority's web portal and in newspapers.

The purpose is to allow anyone having a legitimate objection or claim to raise it.

---

### Step 8 — Objection Period

A period of **14 days** is provided after publication of the notice for objections or claims.

If an objection or claim is received, the application may require further proceedings.

---

### Step 9 — Biometric Verification

If no objection or claim is received, the applicant and legal heirs undergo biometric verification.

Biometric verification may be performed at a notified office in Pakistan or abroad.

If a legal heir's biometric verification cannot be completed, the authority may require that person to appear personally or may use another method to establish identity.

---

### Step 10 — Final Verification

The authority completes the required verification and formalities.

The applicant may be required to appear before the authority on the date fixed for the final stage.

---

### Step 11 — Issuance of Letter of Administration

After satisfactory completion of the required formalities, the authority issues the **Letter of Administration** in favour of the legal heirs.

---

## 6. Fees

According to NADRA's current fee structure:

| Application | Assets ≥ Rs. 100,000 | Assets < Rs. 100,000 |
|---|---:|---:|
| New / Fresh Application | Rs. 20,000 | Rs. 10,000 |
| Duplicate Application | Rs. 5,000 | Rs. 5,000 |
| Amended Application | Rs. 5,000 | Rs. 5,000 |
| Decline on Processed Application | Rs. 15,000 | Rs. 15,000 |
| Office Mistake | Free | Free |

### Sindh Additional Fee

For a new/fresh application involving assets worth **Rs. 100,000 or more**, NADRA's current fee structure lists:

**Rs. 22,000 in Sindh**

instead of the standard Rs. 20,000.

Fees should be verified against the current NADRA fee schedule before payment.

---

## 7. Processing Time

NADRA currently lists the processing time for Succession Certificates and Letters of Administration as:

**14 days after publication**

This means the statutory/public-notice process is an important part of the timeline.

Cases involving objections, incomplete documentation, identity problems, disputes among heirs, or additional verification may take longer.

---

## 8. Important Conditions

The simplified NADRA process is intended for cases where the legal heirs can be established through the required records and there is no unresolved dispute.

If there is a **controversy or dispute among the legal heirs**, the authority may decline to process the application and direct the parties to the appropriate legal forum.

---

## 9. Where to Apply

Depending on the applicable jurisdiction and service availability, applications may be handled through:

- NADRA Succession Facilitation Units
- Notified NADRA offices
- Relevant authority under the applicable provincial succession law

The applicable office depends on the deceased person's residence and/or location of the property.

---

## 10. Letter of Administration vs Succession Certificate

| Feature | Letter of Administration | Succession Certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Immovable property | Movable property |
| Example | House, land, plot | Bank account, shares, securities |
| Applicant | Legal heirs | Legal heirs |
| Death certificate | Required | Required |
| Legal-heir information | Required | Required |
| Identity verification | Required | Required |
| Public notice | Applicable | Applicable |
| NADRA processing | Available under applicable laws | Available under applicable laws |

---

## 11. Important Notes

- A Letter of Administration is generally associated with **immovable property**.
- All relevant legal heirs should be disclosed.
- One legal heir may be authorized to submit the application on behalf of the others.
- NADRA may verify the legal-heir relationship using its records.
- Biometric verification is an important part of the process.
- An objection or dispute can prevent completion through the simplified administrative process.
- Additional court/legal proceedings may be required in disputed cases.
- Requirements and procedures can differ between provinces.
