# Complaints & Dispute-Resolution Services — FBR

## 1. File a Complaint (General / Pakistan Citizen Portal)

**Overview:** For general grievances about FBR service delivery or staff conduct that don't necessarily involve a formal tax dispute.

**Eligibility**
- Any taxpayer or member of the public with a grievance concerning FBR/Revenue Division service delivery

**Requirements**
- Basic complainant details (CNIC, contact information) and a description of the issue

**Process**
1. Use the "File Your Complaint" link on fbr.gov.pk, or lodge the complaint via the Pakistan Citizen Portal (PCP), a cross-government grievance platform.
2. Provide complaint details and supporting documents, if any.
3. The complaint is routed to the relevant FBR wing/field office for response.

**Fees**
- Free

## 2. Federal Tax Ombudsman (FTO)

**Overview:** An independent statutory office (established 1983 under the Constitution) whose main function is to ensure complaints of tax "maladministration" — unjust, unfair, or improper actions by FBR/Revenue Division employees — are investigated promptly and fairly, and to rectify injustice done to a taxpayer.

**Eligibility**
- Any taxpayer who believes they have suffered maladministration by an FBR/tax employee's action (delay, unfair treatment, procedural unfairness, etc.)

**Requirements**
- A written complaint describing the maladministration, with relevant CNIC/NTN and supporting documents
- (Complaints are generally expected within a reasonable time of the grievance; check the FTO's own site for current procedural rules, as FBR's page defers to the FTO office for full details)

**Process**
1. Visit the Federal Tax Ombudsman's official website (linked from fbr.gov.pk under "Federal Tax Ombudsman (FTO)") for the complaint form and submission channels.
2. Submit the complaint with details of the maladministration alleged.
3. The FTO's office investigates independently of FBR and issues findings/recommendations, which can include directing corrective action.

**Fees**
- Free (Ombudsman services are a citizen grievance-redress mechanism, not a paid legal process)

## 3. Alternate Dispute Resolution (ADR)

**Overview:** A mechanism allowing a taxpayer to refer contentious tax issues (Income Tax, Sales Tax, Customs, or FED) to an independent committee of experts for recommendations, enabling an out-of-court settlement with the tax department, rather than pursuing lengthy litigation/appeals.

**Eligibility**
- Taxpayers with a pending dispute or "hardship" case relating to Income Tax, Sales Tax, Customs, or Federal Excise Duty
- Note: for State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs), the normal right of appeal has in some periods been withdrawn in favor of mandatory referral to ADR — check current rules for the applicable category

**Requirements**
- A written application to the FBR Chairman/Board per the prescribed ADR application form (per Rule 231 of the Income Tax Rules, 2001, and equivalent Sales Tax/Customs/FED rules)
- Details of the specific dispute(s) — a taxpayer with multiple disputes may refer all or any number of them to ADR
- If the matter is under appeal, the applicant/relevant Commissioner may need to withdraw the pending appeal for the ADR Committee to take up the case (per current procedure)

**Application Process**
1. Submit the ADR application to the FBR Chairman, identifying the dispute(s) to be referred (not merely the assessment order).
2. FBR forms an Alternate Dispute Resolution Committee (ADRC), which may include independent experts and be headed by a retired judge, per the applicable rules.
3. If a pending appeal exists, it may be withdrawn from the appellate authority/court once the Committee is formed.
4. The Committee examines the dispute, may consult records, and issues recommendations within a prescribed period (historically around 120 days).
5. The Committee's recommendations are forwarded to FBR; the resulting decision is binding on both the taxpayer and FBR once accepted per the rules in force.

**Fees / Costs**
- There is no fee charged to the taxpayer to apply for ADR.
- (Historically, ADR Committee members have been remunerated by FBR out of its own budget — e.g., a retired-judge chair and members paid a percentage of the amount recovered/settled or a fixed sum, whichever is lower — this is an FBR-side cost, not charged to the applicant.)
- No time limit has historically applied for applying to ADR, though this should be reconfirmed against the current ADR rules in force, as procedures are periodically revised.

**Notes**
- Criminal proceedings and certain other categories of cases are generally excluded from ADR and continue through the standard legal process.
- The Taxpayers' Facilitation Guide "The Mechanism of Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)," published by FBR's FATE Wing, provides a detailed FAQ-style walkthrough and is available as a downloadable brochure on the FBR site.
