# How to Become a Filer — FBR

**Overview:** "Filer" status (appearing on the Active Taxpayer List) is achieved by registering with FBR and then filing an income tax return for the relevant tax year — registration alone does not make someone a filer.

**Eligibility**
- Any person with a valid CNIC (individual) or business registration (AOP/company) who registers with FBR and subsequently files a return
- Overseas Pakistanis using NICOP/Passport instead of CNIC

**Requirements**
1. NTN/registration on IRIS (see Income Tax Registration in the Income Tax file)
2. Income details for the relevant tax year (salary certificate, business income records, bank statements)
3. Wealth statement details (assets and liabilities) — mandatory for individuals filing returns
4. Access to IRIS login credentials

**Step-by-Step Process**
1. **Register:** Create an IRIS profile using CNIC/NICOP (if not already registered) — this generates an NTN.
2. **Gather records:** Compile income, expense, and asset/liability details for the tax year.
3. **Log in to IRIS** (iris.fbr.gov.pk) and go to Declaration → Income Tax Return → select the Tax Year.
4. **Complete the return:** Enter income, deductions, tax credits, and wealth statement.
5. **Settle any tax due:** Generate a PSID for the balance payable and pay via bank/online channel.
6. **Submit** the return electronically.
7. **Confirm ATL inclusion:** Check status via the Online Verification Portal or by SMS ("ATL" + CNIC to 9966) — the ATL updates weekly, typically every Sunday.
8. If filing after the annual deadline, pay the applicable ATL surcharge (Rs. 1,000 for individuals, Rs. 10,000 for AOPs, Rs. 20,000 for companies) to be restored to the list, alongside filing the return itself.

**Fees**
- No fee for registration or filing itself, beyond any tax actually owed.
- Late filers pay the statutory penalty/surcharge described in the Income Tax file, plus the ATL restoration surcharge noted above.

**Why It Matters**
- Filers are charged lower withholding tax rates than non-filers on banking transactions, cash withdrawals, vehicle registration, property transactions, dividends, securities, and prize bonds.
- Since the Finance Act 2024, a person who files after the deadline may be treated as a "late filer" — a distinct middle category from an on-time filer, still facing higher advance tax rates on certain property transactions (Sections 236C/236K) even after being restored to the ATL.
