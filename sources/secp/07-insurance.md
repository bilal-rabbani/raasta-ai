# SECP Insurance Sector Licensing

## Overview
SECP is the primary regulator of Pakistan's insurance industry (life, non-life/general, and takaful/window takaful) under the **Insurance Ordinance, 2000**, the **Insurance Rules, 2017** (and earlier 2002 rules as amended), and the **Takaful Rules, 2012**. Any entity wishing to transact insurance/reinsurance business, insurance broking, surveying, or third-party administration in Pakistan must obtain the relevant registration/license from SECP's Insurance Division before commencing operations.

## Categories of Insurance Licenses
1. **Insurance Companies** (life, non-life/general, takaful/general takaful, family takaful)
2. **Window Takaful Operators** (conventional insurers offering Shariah-compliant takaful "windows")
3. **Insurance Brokers**
4. **Insurance Surveyors / Loss Adjusters / Loss Assessors**
5. **Authorized Surveying Officers**
6. **Third Party Administrators (TPAs)** — typically for health insurance claims administration

## 1. Insurance Companies
### Eligibility / Requirements for Registration (Section 6, Insurance Ordinance, 2000)
- Must be incorporated as a public limited company under the Companies Act, 2017.
- **Minimum paid-up capital** (current baseline, phased in since 2015–2017, and subsequently revised further by SECP notification):
  - Non-life insurer / General Takaful operator: **PKR 500 million** (per the SECP (Insurance) Rules amendment referenced by SECP; some sources cite a newly proposed non-life insurer/general takaful minimum of PKR 300 million — applicants should confirm the current figure directly with SECP's Insurance Division, as this has been revised more than once).
  - Life insurer / Family Takaful operator: **PKR 700 million** (again, confirm current SRO).
  - The amount of minimum paid-up capital is calculated **net of any discount** offered on issue of shares.
- Maintain a **statutory deposit equal to 10% of paid-up capital** with the State Bank of Pakistan (under Section 29 of the Insurance Ordinance, 2000).
- Comply with **solvency requirements** under the Insurance Ordinance, 2000 and rules made thereunder.
- Demonstrate ability to meet, and continue to meet, its liabilities.
- Comply with **The Insurance Companies (Sound and Prudent Management) Regulations, 2012** (fit-and-proper criteria for directors/CEO/key officers).
- Appoint a **statutory auditor** approved by SECP.
- Appoint an **actuary** if proposing to carry on life insurance business.
- Maintain **reinsurance treaty arrangements** with "A"-rated reinsurers or better (Section 41, Insurance Ordinance, 2000).

### Documents Required
- Certified true copy of the Certificate of Incorporation (attested by the concerned CRO)
- Certified true copy of Memorandum and Articles of Association
- Auditors' certificate confirming paid-up capital
- SBP statement (not more than 7 days old) confirming the statutory deposit
- Copies of reinsurance treaty arrangements
- Form 29 (directorship particulars/changes, post-incorporation)
- Audited annual accounts of the parent company (where relevant) for prior years, if the applicant is a subsidiary/associate of an existing group

### Process
1. Incorporate as a public limited company with SECP (Company Registration Office).
2. Apply to the Insurance Division of SECP for a Registration Certificate under Section 6 of the Insurance Ordinance, 2000, attaching the documents above.
3. Deposit the statutory deposit with SBP and submit evidence.
4. SECP reviews for compliance with solvency, sound & prudent management, and fit-and-proper requirements.
5. On approval, SECP issues the Certificate of Registration authorizing the company to transact the specified class(es) of insurance business.

## 2. Insurance Brokers
### Eligibility (Section 102, Insurance Ordinance, 2000; Rule 37, Insurance Rules)
- Must be registered as a company — **no individual may be licensed as an insurance broker**.
- **Minimum paid-up share capital:** not less than **PKR 10–15 million** for local brokers (figures vary slightly by source/date of rule) and **USD 0.3–1 million** for a foreign insurance broker seeking registration in Pakistan.
- **Statutory deposit:** cash or approved securities equal to 10% of the minimum paid-up capital (commonly cited as PKR 0.5 million) maintained with SBP.
- **Professional indemnity insurance** of at least PKR 10 million per occurrence.
- At least two employees (one of whom must be a director) with a minimum of five years' relevant experience.
- Declarations of compliance with the Insurance Ordinance, 2000 and applicable rules, and disclosure of shareholding patterns/details of shareholders holding more than 10% of issued capital.

### Process
1. Incorporate the broking entity as a company.
2. Apply to SECP with the prescribed documents (shareholding details, director qualifications/experience, declarations, evidence of statutory deposit and professional indemnity cover).
3. SECP may cancel, refuse to issue, or refuse to renew a license where the broker fails to maintain the prescribed minimum capital, statutory deposit, or professional indemnity insurance, or otherwise contravenes the Ordinance.

## 3. Insurance Surveyors, Loss Adjusters & Loss Assessors
- Licensed under **Section 112 of the Insurance Ordinance, 2000** (excluding adjusters of aviation or maritime losses, who fall under separate categories).
- Categorized into classes as prescribed by SECP (including "Authorized Surveying Officer").
- Requires evidence of relevant qualifications/experience and, where the applicant is a body corporate, a statement of paid-up capital verified by a Chartered Accountant.

## 4. Reinsurance Brokers
- Regulated under the **Securities & Exchange Commission (Re-insurance Brokers) Regulations, 2021**, issued under Section 167 of the Insurance Ordinance, 2000 — covering eligibility, registration requirements, minimum paid-up capital, statutory deposits, record-keeping, and fit-and-proper criteria for CEO/directors.

## Ongoing Obligations
- Periodic (quarterly/annual) financial reporting to SECP.
- Policy wording for life insurance contracts (ordinary life, capital redemption, pension fund, accident & health business) requires prior submission to and approval by SECP before use (Sections 6 and 13, Insurance Ordinance, 2000); non-life policy wordings generally do not require pre-issuance SECP review.
- Continued compliance with solvency margins and sound & prudent management standards; failure can lead to license cancellation.

## Fees
Registration/license fees for insurers, brokers, and surveyors are prescribed by SECP notification and vary by license category; applicants should obtain the current fee schedule from SECP's Insurance Division or the Seventh Schedule/insurance-specific SRO in force at the time of application.
