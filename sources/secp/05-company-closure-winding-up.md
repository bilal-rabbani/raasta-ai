# SECP Company Closure / Winding Up

## Overview
A company registered with SECP can be closed through one of several distinct legal routes depending on its status, assets, and liabilities: **Easy Exit** (for dormant/defunct companies), **voluntary winding up**, or **compulsory/court-ordered winding up** ("liquidation"). Winding up is defined by SECP as the proceedings by which a company is dissolved — its assets are realized, debts are paid off from realized assets or contributories, and any surplus is distributed to members according to shareholding.

## Route 1: Easy Exit (Companies Easy Exit Regulations)
### What It Is
A simplified, low-cost mechanism (originally introduced under the **Companies (Easy Exit) Regulations, 2014**, and re-launched/updated under later SECP notifications, referenced under **Section 426 of the Companies Act, 2017**) allowing an eligible company to have its name struck off the register without undergoing formal winding up.

### Eligibility
- Private companies
- Public unlisted companies
- Associations not for profit licensed under Section 42 of the Companies Ordinance, 1984 / Companies Act, 2017
- The company must have **ceased operations** and have **no significant assets or liabilities**.

### Not Eligible
- Listed companies
- Subsidiaries of listed companies
- Foreign companies (a foreign company wishing to cease Pakistan operations must use the formal winding-up process instead)

### Process & Documents
Applicants must submit an application to the Registrar comprising:
- Company's name and CUIN
- Members' resolution (approving the easy exit/strike-off)
- Declaration/Indemnity from directors/members
- Auditors' Certificate confirming no significant assets/liabilities and no pending litigation/claims
- Confirmation that the company's statutory filings (annual returns, etc.) are up to date, or clearance of any outstanding defaults

### Outcome
If SECP is satisfied, the company's name is struck off the Register of Companies, and the company stands dissolved without a formal court-supervised liquidation.

## Route 2: Voluntary Winding Up
### When Used
Chosen by members/creditors of a solvent or insolvent company that wishes to wind up outside Easy Exit eligibility (e.g., it has assets/liabilities to settle) but without a court order forcing it.

### Process Outline
1. Special resolution of members (or, for insolvent voluntary winding up, in accordance with creditors' involvement) to wind up the company.
2. Appointment of a liquidator to take charge of the company's affairs.
3. Realization of assets and settlement of liabilities/debts by the liquidator.
4. Distribution of any surplus among members in proportion to shareholding.
5. Filing of periodic returns/statements with SECP/Registrar during the winding-up period.
6. Final dissolution once winding up is complete and the Registrar strikes the company off the register.

## Route 3: Compulsory (Court-Ordered) Winding Up
- Initiated by a petition to the relevant High Court (or by SECP itself in specified circumstances) — for example, on grounds of inability to pay debts, or where it is just and equitable to wind up the company.
- The Court may appoint a **provisional manager/liquidator** to manage the company's affairs during proceedings.
- **Filing of a winding-up petition does not mean the company has ceased to exist** — SECP notes that the company continues as a corporate entity, with the change being that its management/administration passes to the liquidator/provisional manager until final dissolution.
- **Revival:** A company under winding up may initiate a revival process before winding up is completed; SECP has stated it supports revival efforts consistent with judicial guidance and the Companies Act, 2017.
- Winding-up proceedings continue unless formally stayed by the Court (Section 313 of the Companies Act, 2017) or by SECP (for Commission-initiated winding up).

## Mergers/Amalgamations (Related Process)
SECP also regulates schemes of **merger and amalgamation** under the Companies Act, 2017 read with Regulation 60 of the Companies Regulations, 2024, which is a distinct process from winding up but is often addressed in the same SECP guidance (see SECP's "FAQs on Mergers, Amalgamations & Winding Up").

## Key Considerations Before Closing a Company
- Clear any outstanding SECP filings (annual returns/Form A, Form 29) and fees/penalties before applying — a company in default typically cannot cleanly proceed to easy exit or winding up.
- Settle tax matters with FBR (NTN de-registration/tax clearance) and provincial tax authorities.
- Address employee-related liabilities (EOBI, social security, gratuity/final settlements) if the company had employees.
- Close corporate bank accounts and settle any charges/mortgages registered against company assets.
